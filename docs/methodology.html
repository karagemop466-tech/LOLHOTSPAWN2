Method & verification

How the picks are made

Fixtures come from Hotspawn. Probabilities never do. Each row is priced from external betting lines when they exist, otherwise from our own Elo model trained on recorded match results.

1. Fixture list

We read Hotspawn's public lobby state (the same JSON the Pick'em page loads) and keep the League of Legends fixtures with their teams, competition, best-of format, stage, tier badge and start time. Start times are taken from Hotspawn's own epoch timestamp in UTC — never from the visitor's local rendering. Fixtures without a published Hotspawn match page are still listed and flagged NO_HOTSPAWN_MATCH_PAGE so no dead link is produced.

2. External market consensus

For each fixture we look for two-way prices from established bookmakers and prediction markets. Decimal odds are converted to implied probabilities and then de-vigged: the two implied probabilities are normalised so they sum to 1, removing the bookmaker margin. Multiple sources are combined by median when at least three agree, otherwise by a fixed-quality weighted mean (Pinnacle 1.0, Bovada 0.8, Polymarket 0.7; other books 0.6).

Access note: Polymarket's homepage (checked 2026-10-23, polymarket.com) states that trading is blocked in the United States and offers a view-only mode. Its prices are used here only as public reference data; this site does not place or recommend bets.

  • The displayed dispersion is the gap between the highest and lowest source probability. ≤6 points = High confidence, ≤10 = Medium, more = Low.
  • Matching a bookmaker row to a Hotspawn fixture uses team-name aliases plus the closest start time within ±36 hours. Bookmakers publish drifting start times; the observed delta is shown on each match page and delta >1.5 h sets START_TIME_DISAGREES_WITH_BOOKS.
  • Bookmaker prices are used as published; no line is invented, smoothed or extrapolated to another match.

3. Our Elo model (when no external line exists)

A chronological Elo rating is computed from Leaguepedia's recorded match results over a two-year window. Only completed matches with a recorded winner are used. Updates are weighted by format and score margin.

Not an official results source: Leaguepedia is a community-maintained database, not Riot or an event organizer. The model probabilities are our estimates from those records, not official odds or organizer-verified win probabilities. The Hotspawn source verifies the fixture details only. Where an exact results page cannot be linked, the historical evidence is not independently verifiable from the match row; treat it as a limitation, not a confirmed official result.
  • Base K 24, scaled ×0.8 for Bo1, ×1.0 for Bo3, ×1.3 for Bo5+; score-margin multiplier up to ×1.5.
  • Activity decay: a team's rating drifts back toward its competition base with a 365-day half-life while inactive.
  • Competition-strength priors: pooling every league in one Elo pool without cross-play inflates teams that farm wins in weak or academy leagues. Each team is therefore anchored to the tier of the competition it actually plays in (international > top regional leagues > EMEA/ERLs > national divisions > academy/amateur). This is a documented prior, not a measured quantity — see the limitation below.
  • Shrinkage: with few games, the Elo probability is pulled toward 50/50 (shrink = games/(games+8)).
  • Head-to-head: a smoothed H2H term contributes at most 15% and only with at least three meetings.
  • One missing side: the competition median rating (or tier prior) is substituted and the shrink is halved; the row is labelled MODEL_LEAGUE_PRIOR.
  • No history at all: the row is unresolved (50/50 placeholder) and listed on its own page.

4. Out-of-sample validation

To show how the model actually performs, ratings are fitted on data before the last 30 days, frozen, and then scored on the matches played in those 30 days (only matches where both teams already had a rating). Log-loss and Brier score are compared with a coin-flip baseline; accuracy is the share of matches where the higher-probability side won.

5. What we refuse to do

  • Hotspawn's crowd percentage is never an input. It is crowd behaviour, not a probability, and is displayed only as context.
  • No inferred values. If a price, result or time cannot be read from a linked source, it stays blank or the row is flagged — never filled from memory or pattern-matching.
  • No silent edits. Irregularities are published with the source link on Flagged for review.

Update cadence & verification

The pipeline runs on a GitHub Actions schedule every six hours: it fetches the Hotspawn fixture list, external prices and Leaguepedia results, rebuilds the model, verifies that every fixture has either a market line or a model output, and publishes data/picks.json. Each run records its source status above; if a fetch fails the run is flagged rather than publishing partial or invented numbers. Every table row links the exact page used for its values.
